Pre-Tax vs. Post-Tax Tipping in the US

In the United States, traditional dining etiquette—and what most hospitality guides recommend—is to calculate your tip on the pre-tax subtotal. Because sales tax is collected for city and state budgets rather than restaurant wages, using a pre-tax tip calculator ties your gratuity directly to the food, drinks, and service you received.

Why do printed "Suggested Tip" lines at the bottom of many receipts look higher? A large share of point-of-sale terminals compute those suggested 18%, 20%, and 22% numbers on the post-tax total. In cities with 9% to 11% restaurant taxes—like Chicago, Seattle, or Los Angeles—tipping 20% after tax effectively works out to nearly 22% on the pre-tax check.

Either approach is fine if you prefer to be generous or want simpler math. Use the toggle in this restaurant tip calculator by percentage to see both numbers side by side before signing the receipt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should you tip before or after sales tax in the US?
Standard US dining etiquette is to calculate your tip on the pre-tax subtotal. Sales tax goes to your city or state government rather than the restaurant or your server, so tipping on the food and drink subtotal is the traditional benchmark. That said, tipping on the post-tax total is also common—especially when people double the tax or use printed receipt suggestions—and adds roughly 1% to 2% to the bill.
How do you split a restaurant bill evenly with tax and tip?
Start with the pre-tax subtotal from your receipt, add the sales tax (either as a percentage or the exact dollar amount printed on the check), calculate the tip on your preferred base, and divide the combined grand total by the number of diners. This calculator breaks out tax per person, tip per person, and the exact total each person owes.
What is the standard restaurant tip percentage in the US?
For sit-down table service in the United States, 18% to 20% of the pre-tax subtotal is the standard range for good service. Diners typically leave 15% for basic or adequate service and 22% to 25% for standout service, complex orders, or busy holiday shifts.
What if the restaurant already added an automatic gratuity for a large group?
Many US restaurants automatically add an 18% or 20% service charge for parties of six or more. Check the bottom of your receipt before entering a tip percentage. If an automatic gratuity is already included in the check total, set the tip percentage to 0% (or only add a small extra percentage like 2% to 5% if you want to leave more) so your group does not accidentally double-tip.
How does the round up to the nearest dollar option work?
When you turn on Round Up Per Person, each diner's share is rounded up to the next whole dollar—for example, $38.48 becomes $39.00. This makes Venmo requests and cash splits cleaner, and the extra change simply goes to your server as a small bonus on top of the base tip.
